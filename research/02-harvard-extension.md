# 02 — Harvard Extension School (HES)
**Quota: exactly 12 US semester credits (3 × 4-credit courses)**
Research date: 2026-09-13. Sub-agent #2, Round 1.

## 1. Prose section

### 1.1 Tuition model

HES prices **per credit**, and essentially every HES undergraduate-credit course is **4 credits**.

| Item | 2025–26 (prior yr) | **2026–27 (current)** | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undergraduate credit, per credit | $540 | **$565** | 2026–27 rate confirmed via search of HES tuition pages (2026-09) |
| Undergraduate credit, per 4-cr course | $2,160 | **$2,260** | same |
| On-campus lab fee (lab courses only) | — | $150 | not applicable to our 3 online picks |
| Payment-plan enrollment fee (optional) | $40/term | $40/term (est.) | only if you use the instalment plan |
| Application fee for non-degree course registration | **none** | **none** | open enrollment — no application required |

**There is no separate per-term registration fee published for open-enrollment (non-degree)
students.** Late registration and add/drop fees exist but are avoidable. HES publishes new
academic-year rates each March, so the $2,260 figure is the live 2026–27 rate as of Sept 2026.

**Cost for our 12 credits = 3 × $2,260 = $6,780.**

This is by far the most expensive per-credit slot in the 150.334-credit plan — roughly
$565/credit versus ~$25/credit at ASU ULC. That is the point: this is the **prestige/credibility
slot**, deliberately capped at 12 credits (8% of the plan) while consuming ~a third of the
non-UMPI cash. The line on the transcript reads *Harvard University*.

### 1.2 Open enrollment — confirmed

HES is genuinely open-enrollment for coursework:

- **"There is no application required to enroll in classes at Harvard Extension School."**
  You create a MyDCE account, create a DCEKey credential, complete pre-registration
  (biographical/contact confirmation, required before *every* term), and register.
- **"You do not have to be an undergraduate student to take a course for undergraduate credit,
  nor are you required to have an undergraduate degree to register for graduate credit."**
  This is the sentence that makes the whole play legal: a non-admitted adult can register for
  **undergraduate credit (UN)** in any course that offers that credit level.
- Courses are offered at one or more **credit levels: undergraduate / graduate / noncredit**,
  stated in each course description. You pick the level at registration. Graduate-level
  registrants get extra or harder assignments; undergraduate level is the cheaper, lighter track
  and is the correct one here (ASU is a *bachelor's* completion).
- Exceptions to open enrollment exist and must be avoided: **capstones, precapstones and
  proseminars are restricted to admitted degree/certificate candidates**, and a few courses carry
  instructor-permission or placement gates. None of our three picks are in those categories.

### 1.4 Delivery formats

Three formats, labelled per course section:
- **Online asynchronous** — no set meeting time, weekly deadlines. *All three picks are this.*
- **Online synchronous ("live web")** — a fixed weekly meeting time over Zoom. Real conflict risk
  for a full-time-employed student (e.g. ENVR E-119E Sustainable Infrastructure meets Wed 5:30–7:30 PM).
- **On campus** — Cambridge. Avoid entirely; also triggers the $150 lab fee on lab courses.

The **on-campus weekend requirement** people associate with HES belongs to the *ALB degree
program*, not to individual course registration. As a non-degree open-enrollment student we never
touch it.

### 1.5 Transferability to ASU

- HES is accredited by the **New England Commission of Higher Education (NECHE)** as part of
  Harvard University. Credit appears on a **Harvard University** transcript.
- **ASU accepts transfer credit for traditional coursework completed at regionally accredited
  colleges and universities**, for college-level (non-remedial) courses with a grade of
  **C– or higher**.
- HES's own language: "Harvard Extension School courses are accepted toward degrees at most
  colleges and universities… confirm your prospective school's policy before enrolling."
- **Expect elective / general-transfer credit, not a course-by-course ASU equivalency.** HES is
  unlikely to have pre-built entries in ASU's Transfer Guide, so these will land as
  *departmental elective* credit evaluated by the ASU transfer office. For a **BA in General
  Studies** — which is elective-heavy and focus-area-driven — that is exactly what we want; the
  courses should be usable as focus-area electives with an advisor's sign-off.
- Semester-credit math is 1:1 (HES already uses US semester credits). 3 × 4 = **12.000**.
- *(Per the shared brief, ASU's 30-credit residency requirement is treated as WAIVED. That is a
  user-imposed modelling assumption, not real ASU policy.)*

### 1.6 Why HES owns these subjects

The other five platforms in this plan cannot produce any of the following:

- **Harvard FAS / HKS ladder faculty teaching the actual course.** GOVT E-1722 is taught by
  **Dustin Tingley, PhD, Professor of Government, Harvard University** (also affiliated with
  Harvard's Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability). PHIL E-150 is taught by
  **Mathias Risse, PhD, Berthold Beitz Professor in Human Rights, Global Affairs and Philosophy,
  Harvard Kennedy School** — one of the field's actual authorities on the ethics and governance of AI.
  SNHU, UMPI, BYU-IS and ASU ULC have nothing comparable; UCLA Extension's strength is
  practitioner-taught engineering, not policy theory.
- **Government / public policy depth.** A full GOVT department with US politics, IR, comparative
  politics, political philosophy and public policy — the exact spine of focus area 1.
- **Environmental policy, law and sustainability.** A large ENVR department behind the
  Sustainability master's and the Environmental Policy & International Development certificate —
  the *policy and regulatory* layer that sits on top of UCLA Extension's power-electronics and
  grid engineering. Together they produce a genuine socio-technical profile rather than two
  disconnected halves.
- **Ethics of technology as a first-class academic subject**, not a business-school module.

**Division of labour inside the 150-credit plan:** UCLA Extension supplies the converters,
inverters and grid integration; HES supplies the law, politics and ethics that decide whether any
of it gets built. That contrast is the story the whole degree tells.

### Course notes

**1. PHIL E-150 — Philosophy of Technology: From Marx and Heidegger to Artificial Intelligence**
Fully verified. 4 credits; offered at **Undergraduate, Graduate and Noncredit** levels — register
**UN**. Online asynchronous. Spring term (the 2025–26 section ran 1/26–5/16; Spring 2027 dates
would be Jan 25 – May 15, 2027). Instructor **Mathias Risse (Harvard Kennedy School)**.
**No prerequisites published.** This is the single best "technology & government" artifact in the
whole 150-credit plan: a Harvard Kennedy School human-rights/AI-governance professor teaching the
philosophical foundations of technology from Marx and Heidegger through contemporary AI. Maps to
the Technology & Government focus area and is a plausible ASU **HU** (humanities) gen-ed as a
bonus.

**2. GOVT E-1722 — The Politics of Climate Change and the Environment**
Fully verified as to code, title, 4 credits, **available for both undergraduate and graduate
credit** (register UN), online asynchronous delivery, and instructor **Dustin Tingley**.
**No prerequisites published.** Historically offered in both Fall and Spring terms (Fall 2023 and
Spring 2023 syllabi both exist), which gives scheduling slack. The catalog carries an explicit
**exclusion: you may not count both GOVT E-1722 and GOVT S-1511 (Harvard Summer School version)
for degree or certificate credit** — take one, not both.
This course is the hinge of the whole plan: it is a **GOVT-department** course, so it books
cleanly against **Technology & Government**, while its content (energy transition, decarbonisation
politics, environmental governance) directly feeds the **Socio-Technical Energy & Environment**
focus. Either focus area can claim it.

**3. ENVR E-199 — Environmental Law and Policy**
**Partly verified.** The code `ENVR E-199`, the title, and section 1 / **CRN 17015** were
confirmed, and a Fall Term 2024 syllabus for `ENVR E-199-1 Environmental Law and Policy` exists,
so the course is real and fall-scheduled. Content: the major areas of US environmental and natural
resources law and policy — water quality and quantity, biodiversity, hazardous waste and toxic
substances, land use.
**NOT confirmed:** the credit hours (assumed 4, the HES standard) and — more importantly —
**whether undergraduate credit is offered**. HES 100-level courses are conventionally available at
both undergraduate and graduate credit, and ENVR E-199 is not a capstone/precapstone/proseminar
(the three restricted categories), but this must be checked on the live course page before
registering. Fallbacks with verified codes if ENVR E-199 turns out to be graduate-credit-only:
**ENVR E-119D Zero Energy and Passive Buildings** (CRN 24776 — grid fundamentals, building energy
dynamics, renewable systems, energy economics, policy, codes, financing; an even better fit for
the power/grid slant) or **ENVR E-119E Sustainable Infrastructure** (verified, but it meets **live
on Wednesdays 5:30–7:30 PM**, a synchronous-conflict risk for a full-time-employed student).

## 3. Prerequisites, restrictions and timeline risks

- **Prerequisites:** none published for PHIL E-150 or GOVT E-1722. Unknown (assumed none) for
  ENVR E-199. HES policy-side courses in these departments are typically open.
- **No mandatory on-campus component** in any of the three picks. The HES "on-campus weekend"
  requirement belongs to the ALB degree program, which we are not entering.
- **No synchronous component** in PHIL E-150 or GOVT E-1722 (both confirmed online asynchronous).
  ENVR E-199's format is unconfirmed — verify it is not live-web before registering.
- **No degree-candidate-only restriction** applies: none of the three is a capstone, precapstone
  or proseminar. Registration is open-enrollment with pre-registration completed each term.
- **Register at the UN (undergraduate) credit level.** Registering GR would cost the same but
  produce graduate credit, which does **not** transfer into an ASU bachelor's cleanly.
- **Grade floor:** ASU requires **C– or better** to transfer. Harvard grading is genuinely
  demanding; these are not credit-farming courses.
- **Calendar friction is the real risk.** HES full terms are ~15 weeks and 4-credit courses are
  heavy. Two of our three are spring-weighted and one is fall-weighted, so 12 HES credits
  realistically span **two full terms across ~a calendar year** (e.g. Spring 2027: PHIL E-150;
  Fall 2027: GOVT E-1722 + ENVR E-199). HES cannot be compressed the way ULC, SNHU 8-week terms,
  or UMPI competency sessions can. HES is therefore the **pacing constraint** on the whole
  150.334-credit schedule, not ULC or UMPI.
