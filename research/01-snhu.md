## 1. Why SNHU owns this slice of the plan

SNHU Online is the cheapest *regionally accredited, transcript-clean, 8-week-term* source of
**applied upper-division public-administration and cybersecurity-policy credit** in the six-school
fleet. It is not a research university and should not be asked to carry theory, math or engineering —
UCLA Extension owns power electronics, HES owns the prestige policy/enviro depth, BYU-IS and ASU ULC
own gen-ed, UMPI owns bulk business. What SNHU uniquely gives us is:

- **A real undergraduate Public Administration teaching line.** SNHU runs both a BS-in-Business-Administration
  *Public Administration concentration* and a standalone **BA in Public Administration** online, so the
  300-level `PAD` sequence (`PAD 330`, `PAD 340`, `PAD 345`, `PAD 410`) actually exists and actually runs
  every 8 weeks — most cheap online providers have no undergraduate PAD line at all.
- **A cybersecurity line whose 200-level entry point has no prerequisites**, including a course
  (`CYB 260`) that is explicitly about *law, regulation, ethics and human factors* rather than tooling.
  That is exactly the "Technology & Government" intersection and does **not** duplicate UCLA Extension's
  hardware/grid coursework.
- **Six 8-week terms per year with rolling, no-transcript-required non-degree enrolment**, so these four
  courses can be dropped into almost any gap in the Round-2 timeline.

**Deliberate non-choices.** `QSO 340 Project Management` is SNHU's signature applied course and was the
obvious 4th pick, but published syllabi list **`MAT 240 Applied Statistics` as a prerequisite**, which
would either burn a slot or create a cross-school prereq dependency; UMPI YourPace can deliver project
management inside its flat-rate block for ~$0 marginal cost. Likewise `BUS 210`, `IT 200` and
`COM 315` were dropped as too generic or too duplicative of UMPI.

## 3. Enrolment / admission — can a non-matriculated student do this?

**Yes, cleanly.** SNHU markets "individual classes" / "online courses for college credit" as a first-class
product (~500 transfer-friendly courses). Key facts gathered:

- A student may take **up to 10 courses without enrolling in a degree program** (our plan uses 4).
- **No high-school diploma or GED is required** to register for individual courses; **no transcripts,
  no essay, no test scores, no application fee**.
- Registration is open **up to the day before a term starts**.
- Credits appear on a normal SNHU transcript and are marketed as transferable to SNHU *or another
  university, subject to that university's transfer policy*.
- Course numbering: **100–299 = lower division, 300–499 = upper division.** Non-degree students are not
  documented as being blocked from 300-level courses, but **prerequisite enforcement at registration is
  the open question** (see Risks).
- Financial aid is unavailable for individual-course-only students → this is a cash purchase.

## 5. Transfer out to ASU

- SNHU is **institutionally (regionally) accredited by NECHE** — the same accreditation class ASU
  requires for transfer credit.
- **ASU accepts an unlimited number of transfer credits from four-year regionally accredited
  institutions** (the 64-credit cap applies to community-college/lower-division work, not to SNHU).
- ASU's stated residency rule is **30 of the final 64 credits in residence**; per the shared brief this
  is **treated as WAIVED by user instruction** (a modeling assumption, *not* real ASU policy).
- The **BA in General Studies requires 120 credits with a 45-credit upper-division minimum.** Two of the
  four courses here (`PAD 330`, `PAD 345`) are 300-level and should post as **upper division**;
  `CYB 200`/`CYB 260` are 200-level lower division. If Round 2 finds the fleet short on upper-division
  credit, swap `CYB 200`+`CYB 260` for `PAD 340 Public Fiscal Management` + `PAD 410` (program
  development & evaluation) to make all 12 credits upper division — at the cost of narrowing the
  topical spread and adding 400-level prereq risk.
- SNHU courses with **no direct ASU equivalent post as elective / departmental credit** — which is fine
  for a General Studies BA whose focus areas are satisfied by approved-elective lists, but the student
  should run each course through **transferguide.apps.asu.edu** (or a MyPath2ASU plan) before paying.

### Why these four, in one line each
1. **PAD 330 Public Administration** — the anchor: theory of public administration, *the role of government
   and regulation in public policy development*, and administrative ethics. This is the literal core of the
   "Technology & Government" focus area and nothing else in the fleet duplicates it.
2. **PAD 345 Policy Analysis and the Role of the Public** — the policy *process* course: stages of the public
   policy cycle, how the public influences policy, stakeholder management. Pairs with HES's deeper policy
   work rather than repeating it.
3. **CYB 200 Cybersecurity Foundations** — zero-prereq on-ramp; covers adversarial vs. environmental threats
   *and explicitly the legal and human factors that shape organisational security strategy*. Also the required
   gate for course 4.
4. **CYB 260 Legal and Human Factors of Cybersecurity** — the true tech-policy capstone of this block:
   privacy/compliance law, ethics, and human-centred attack surfaces. This is "technology regulation" taught
   as a technology course, which is precisely the degree's first focus area.

### Suggested internal order (Round 2 input)
`CYB 200` and `PAD 330` are both prereq-free and can run **together in term A**; `CYB 260` (needs CYB 200)
and `PAD 345` (logically after PAD 330) run **together in term B**. Two consecutive 8-week terms clears
all 12 credits.

## 7. Risks

1. **Prerequisite enforcement on non-degree registration is unverified.** `CYB 260` requires `CYB 200`;
   SNHU's registration system may block a non-degree student from a 300/200-level course until the prereq
   posts. Mitigation: sequence CYB 200 first (already planned), and call admissions before paying.
2. **PAD courses may not be on the ~500-course "individual classes" menu.** Verified individual-class pages
   exist for CYB-200, QSO-340, IT-200, PSY-108 etc.; a PAD-330 individual-class page was **not** found in the
   index. If PAD is concentration-only, fall back to `QSO 340 Project Management` (+ satisfy MAT 240
   elsewhere) and/or `COM 315 Communication in the Digital Age`.
3. **Price uncertainty of ±$144.** SNHU's tuition page implies $354/cr for 2026-27 while its own class pages
   still quote $1,026 per 3-cr course ($342/cr). Sources also disagree on the 2025-26 rate ($330 vs $342).
   Budget the higher figure.
4. **Only 6 of 12 credits are upper division.** ASU's BA General Studies needs 45 UD credits; if the fleet
   runs short, swap the CYB pair for `PAD 340` + `PAD 410`.
5. **No ASU equivalency guarantee.** PAD/CYB courses will most likely transfer as elective/departmental
   credit, not as named ASU equivalents. Acceptable for General Studies, but must be pre-checked in ASU's
   transfer guide.
6. **2027-28 calendar is projected, not published.** Any Round-2 plan touching 2028 SNHU terms must be
   re-validated when SNHU posts the 2027-28 calendar (typically ~12 months ahead).
7. **Research was snippet-based** (egress proxy blocked all direct fetches). Every figure here should be
   re-confirmed against the live site before enrolment.
